### Pangenomics and Complex Genomic Variation

I use high-quality haplotype-resolved assemblies within a pangenome framework to resolve complex variation that remains difficult to characterize with short-read (NGS) sequencing and conventional reference-based approaches. Functional evidence from multiple omics layers helps explain the molecular consequences of this [sequence-resolved variation](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.27.740875).

### Evolutionary Genomics and Medicine

Tracing the origins of complex structural variation through comparisons among primate genomes, particularly those of great apes, and examining the contribution of [archaic introgression](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.23.740208) in modern humans. Ancient DNA analyses reconstruct the spread and selection history of these variants, exemplified by the [gene-culture coevolution](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.17.745382) (e.g., dietary adaptation). These evolutionary histories help explain why disease-associated variants arose and persisted, and how past adaptation relates to present-day disease susceptibility.

### Genomics for Precision Medicine

Pangenome references derived from high-quality haplotype-resolved assemblies support studies of how complex genomic variation contributes to disease susceptibility. Cohort-scale genotyping enables GWAS/PheWAS to identify [disease-associated structural haplotypes](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.17.745382). I integrate their shared and population-specific association effects with molecular evidence to identify disease-relevant pathways and explain population differences in susceptibility.
