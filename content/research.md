### Pangenomics and Complex Genomic Variation

I use high-quality haplotype-resolved assemblies within a pangenome framework to resolve variation that remains difficult to characterize with short-read sequencing and a single reference genome. Evidence from proteomics, metabolomics and other functional assays helps explain the molecular consequences of this sequence-resolved variation. [East Asian pangenomes](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.27.740875) and [pepsinogen A copy-number variation](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.17.745382) illustrate this approach.

### Evolutionary Genomics and Medicine

Comparisons among primate genomes, particularly those of great apes, help trace the origins of complex structural variation. Within humans, I examine the contribution of [archaic introgression](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.23.740208), then use ancient DNA to reconstruct the spread and selection history of variants, including [dietary adaptation at the PGA locus](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.17.745382). These histories provide a basis for investigating how past adaptation relates to present-day disease susceptibility.

### Genomics for Precision Medicine

Pangenome references make complex structural haplotypes accessible to cohort-scale genotyping and GWAS/PheWAS. By integrating shared and population-specific association effects with molecular evidence, I aim to identify disease-relevant pathways and explain the biological basis of population differences in susceptibility.
